In this September 2026 video, creator Isaac Flath shares what he calls his favorite recent document news: a Davis County, Utah school district case study about using Chandra OCR for STEM accessibility. He explains that teacher Ryan Hunsaker, a certified braille transcriber, needs to produce math and science materials in braille for blind and low-vision students, but state transcription services are often unavailable, especially for mid-year arrivals or late curriculum changes. Previously, Hunsaker tried generic OCR, which left equations missing and paragraphs out of order, requiring hours of manual fixes before importing into braille software. With Chandra, the system produces Markdown and LaTeX from handwritten worksheets (shown as a biology systems diagram), which he then processes through Pandoc into his braille tools. Checking the OCR now takes five to ten minutes instead of hours. Flath highlights that measuring the actual cleanup time is critical to determine if a model is truly useful, calling this a great example of practical AI helping educators.
