Presenter Isaac Flath demonstrates a failure mode in document OCR on a historical U.S. census form. He shows that generic OCR skips a whole section, Docling returns only fragments and damaged labels instead of a numbered grid, and Gemini reconstructs the main grid but stops at question 33, missing seven fields after it (items 34, 35a-c, 36, etc.). By cropping the missing panel and enlarging it, the same model successfully reads the full wording. He notes he already knew where the error was, but processing thousands of completed copies would make the omission invisible. His recommended workflow is to avoid generic OCR and instead start with a blank form to build a reusable template/schema, extract structured data from it, and use that to guide the Vision Language Model (VLM). This also enables automated checks for missing fields against the expected schema. He ends by inviting viewers to comment "OCR" for his guides on choosing OCR models and using VLMs for hard documents like messy scans, tables, and handwriting.
