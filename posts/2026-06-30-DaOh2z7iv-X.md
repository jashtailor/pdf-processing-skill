In this video, creator Isaac Flath (@isaac_flath) explains why a single failed tool call can destroy user trust in AI products, even when everything else works correctly. He uses an example posted on Reddit where a user asked Claude to create a walking route map between two locations in Hongdae, Seoul. The AI successfully performed multiple tool calls—place search, place search, route overview, static route map—but then failed on the final step, returning a message saying 'there's clearly a failure' and that it couldn't drill in to show the map. Flath walks through the user's perspective: after seeing several successful steps, the final failure makes them question whether the earlier searches and route data were valid, whether the AI hallucinated, and whether they can trust any of the information. He notes that while technically the user could just search Google Maps themselves, the broken experience erodes confidence. He concludes by referencing a Reddit comment where someone dug into MCP logs and saw the tool calls were working, but emphasizes that backend logs are not the user experience—users need clear, actionable feedback, not silent failures.
