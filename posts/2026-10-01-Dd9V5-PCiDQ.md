In this 2026-era educational video, creator Isaac Flath (@isaac_flath) demonstrates a common failure mode in optical character recognition (OCR) when processing tables with merged cells, using the United States Government FY 2024 Statement of Operations and Changes in Net Position as an example. He explains that the original PDF has a single '2024' header spanning four columns (Funds other than Dedicated Collections, Funds from Dedicated Collections, Eliminations, Total). When processed by Gemini, the Markdown output incorrectly associates the '2024' year only with the second column ('Funds from Dedicated Collections'), leaving the other three columns without a year label, making it appear the data applies to just one column. He contrasts this with Docling, which corrects the issue by duplicating '2024' into every applicable column header, ensuring clarity that the year applies to all columns. Flath notes that while prompting Gemini Flash might fix this specific case, it's not a reliable approach for flexible handling of merged table cells.
