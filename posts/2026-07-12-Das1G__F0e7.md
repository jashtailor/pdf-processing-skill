A presenter explains a use case for AI-powered flashcard tools, specifically AnkiHub, which provides collaborative, AI-enhanced decks popular with medical students studying for exams. He describes a workflow where a student uploads a lecture PDF and wants the system to find every relevant flashcard in their curated deck and tell them what to study. To do this, the system must parse the PDF, break it into smaller prompts, and run multiple searches—grep/rg, web search, and semantic search—to retrieve matching cards. The retrieved results are aggregated, then a generation step determines relevance based on search expansiveness. He emphasizes that generation only works if the context-building retrieval step succeeds; failures lead to bad answers. A common fix is increasing top-K retrieval (e.g., 50, 100, 150) to capture all cards, but this introduces problems: context rot, compaction limits, performance degradation, increased latency, and higher cost due to more tokens. The core message is that retrieval quality is foundational to useful AI generation in this study-aid scenario.
